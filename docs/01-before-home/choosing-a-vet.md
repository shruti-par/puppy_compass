# Choosing a vet

Your veterinarian is the single most important relationship in your puppy's life after you. A good one is a calm, judgment-free partner for the next decade-plus. It's worth choosing deliberately rather than just picking the closest clinic.

## How to find the right clinic

- **Ask for referrals** from your breeder/rescue, local dog friends, trainers, and groomers. Reputation travels.
- **Look for accreditation.** [AAHA-accredited](https://www.aaha.org) hospitals are voluntarily held to a published standard of care — a useful (not mandatory) signal of quality.
- **Read reviews for patterns,** not one-offs — consistent comments about communication, wait times, and how staff handle anxious animals tell you more than any single review.
- **Consider logistics:** distance (you'll visit a lot in the first months), hours, and whether they handle their own emergencies or refer out.

!!! tip "Find the ER *before* you need it"
    Identify the nearest **24/7 emergency animal hospital** now and save the
    number. Many day-practice vets are closed nights and weekends, which is
    exactly when puppies find trouble. Put it on your
    [emergency card](../00-start/emergency-card.md).

## Questions to ask when you call or visit

- Do you treat my puppy's **breed/size** regularly?
- What's your approach to **fear-free / low-stress handling**? (A clinic that values this makes a lifetime of vet visits easier.)
- How do you handle **after-hours emergencies**?
- What are your **costs** for the puppy package — exam, vaccine series, fecal test, microchip, spay/neuter?
- Do you offer **puppy wellness plans** that spread the first-year costs out?

## Booking the first visit

Schedule it for **within 48–72 hours of bringing your puppy home.** Bring:

- Any **paperwork** from the breeder/shelter — vaccination and deworming records, and the pedigree or adoption papers.
- A **fresh stool sample** (within ~12 hours) so they can check for intestinal parasites.
- Your **questions**, written down — you'll forget them otherwise.

## What the first appointment should cover

This visit sets the medical baseline. Expect your vet to:

1. **Weigh** your puppy and do a **nose-to-tail physical** — heart, lungs, eyes, ears, teeth/bite, belly, joints, skin, and a check for things like a heart murmur, hernia, or retained baby teeth.
2. **Review the vaccine and deworming records** and map out the schedule — see [Vaccines & deworming](../02-first-weeks/vaccines.md) and build a dated plan with the [planner tool](../06-tools/vaccine-planner.md).
3. **Run a fecal test** for worms and start/continue deworming.
4. **Discuss parasite prevention** — heartworm, fleas, and ticks (see [Parasites](../05-health/parasites.md)).
5. **Microchip** (if not done) and talk about registration.
6. **Talk nutrition** — confirm a complete-and-balanced, AAFCO-appropriate puppy food and the right amount for your puppy's projected size.
7. **Plan spay/neuter timing** — this is increasingly **size- and breed-specific** (see [Spay & neuter](../04-growing-up/spay-neuter.md)), so start the conversation early.

!!! note "Bring up behavior, too"
    Ask about **safe socialization** before the vaccine series is complete,
    teething, and any nipping or housetraining hiccups. Your vet has seen it
    all and can flag anything that's actually a medical issue in disguise.
