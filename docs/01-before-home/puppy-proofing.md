# Puppy-proofing your home

A puppy explores the world with its mouth, and it can fit into, climb onto, and swallow things you'd never expect. The single best move you can make is to **get down on the floor in each room and look around at puppy eye level.** What dangles? What's chewable? What could be swallowed? That's your to-do list.

!!! tip "Size note"
    **Small/toy puppies** can squeeze through gaps as narrow as a few inches
    and fall from heights that look harmless — block stair gaps and never
    leave them on a sofa or bed. **Large-breed puppies** grow into counter-
    surfers fast; assume that by 5–6 months they can reach anything on a
    kitchen counter or low table.

## The universal hazards (every room)

- **Electrical cords and phone chargers** — a classic teething target that can cause mouth burns or electrocution. Bundle, hide, or run them through cord protectors.
- **Small swallowable objects** — coins, hair ties, socks, kids' toys, earbuds, batteries. Button batteries are a true emergency if swallowed.
- **Trash cans** — get lidded or behind-cabinet bins. Garbage is the #1 source of "my dog ate something" vet visits.
- **Anything string-like** — drawstrings, ribbon, dental floss, blind cords. These can cause dangerous intestinal "linear foreign bodies."

## Kitchen

- Use **child-proof latches** on lower cabinets, especially the one under the sink (cleaners, dishwasher pods).
- Keep the **trash secured**.
- Know your **toxic foods** cold — see [Good vs. bad](../02-first-weeks/good-and-bad.md). Xylitol (now often labeled "birch sugar," found in sugar-free gum and some peanut butters), grapes/raisins, onions, garlic, and chocolate are the big ones.

## Living room

- Secure **bookshelves and TVs** to the wall — puppies pull and climb.
- Pick up **remote controls, shoes, and kids' toys** (choking and chewing hazards).
- Move **houseplants** out of reach and check them against a toxic-plant list. Common offenders: lilies, sago palm, pothos, philodendron, aloe, and dieffenbachia.

## Bedrooms & bathrooms

- **Medications** — human pills are among the top pet poisons. One dropped ibuprofen can seriously hurt a puppy. Store everything in closed, high cabinets.
- Keep the **toilet lid down** (cleaning tablets, drowning risk for tiny pups).
- Stow **hair ties, cotton swabs, and laundry** — socks and underwear are swallowed shockingly often.

## Garage, yard & garden

- **Antifreeze (ethylene glycol)** is sweet-tasting and deadly in tiny amounts — clean spills immediately and store sealed.
- Lock away **rodenticides, slug bait, fertilizers, and pesticides.**
- Check the yard for **escape gaps** under fences and for **toxic plants** (azalea, oleander, foxglove, yew). Cocoa mulch can also be dangerous.
- Make sure the yard is genuinely **secure** — puppies dig and slip through smaller gaps than you'd think.

## Set up the "yes" spaces

Proofing isn't only about removing dangers — it's about **deciding where your puppy is allowed to be.** Before day one:

- [ ] Choose a **puppy-safe zone** (often the kitchen or a gated area) with washable floors.
- [ ] Set up the **crate** in a calm corner of a room where the family hangs out — see [Crate training](../02-first-weeks/crate-training.md).
- [ ] Have **baby gates** ready to manage access to stairs and off-limits rooms.
- [ ] Pick up an **ex-pen (exercise pen)** if you want a larger contained area.

!!! warning "The two-week tidy habit"
    Puppy-proofing isn't one-and-done. Puppies get taller, bolder, and
    sneakier every week. Re-scan your "danger floor view" every couple of
    weeks during the first six months.
