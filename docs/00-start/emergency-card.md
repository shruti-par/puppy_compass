## Go to the ER *now* if you see…

These are the signs that mean "car keys, not Google." When in doubt, call ahead so they're ready for you.

- **Trouble breathing** — gasping, blue/grey gums, neck stretched out
- **A bloated, hard belly** with restlessness or unproductive retching (possible [bloat/GDV](../05-health/emergencies.md#bloat) — a true emergency, especially in deep-chested breeds)
- **Collapse, seizures, or unresponsiveness**
- **Pale, white, or blue gums** (press a gum — color should return in ~2 seconds)
- **Repeated vomiting or bloody/black diarrhea**, especially in an under-vaccinated puppy (possible [parvo](../05-health/emergencies.md#parvo))
- **Known or suspected poison eaten** — chocolate, xylitol/birch sugar, grapes/raisins, rat bait, human meds, antifreeze
- **Can't urinate, or straining with nothing coming out**
- **A bite wound, fall, or hit by a car** — even if they seem okay
- **Heatstroke signs** — heavy panting, drooling, wobbliness after heat/exercise
- **Eye injury, or a swollen face/hives** (possible allergic reaction)

## Two things that buy time

1. **Know how to check gum color and capillary refill.** Healthy gums are bubble-gum pink and moist; press, release, and color should refill in about 2 seconds. White, blue, or "tacky" dry gums = call now.
2. **Don't induce vomiting unless a vet or poison control tells you to.** Some substances do more damage coming back up.

## When you call, have this ready

- Your puppy's approximate **weight and age**
- **What happened**, and roughly **when**
- For poisons: **what** they ate, **how much**, and the **packaging** if you have it
- Any **symptoms** you're seeing right now
