# Emergencies — what to do

This is the page to skim *now*, while calm, so the steps are familiar if you ever need them. The overall rule: **when in doubt, call your vet or the nearest 24/7 emergency hospital and go.** It's always better to be seen and sent home than to wait too long.

!!! danger "Have these saved already"
    - **Your vet** and the **nearest 24/7 ER vet** — in your phone, today.
    - **ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435**
    - **Pet Poison Helpline: (855) 764-7661**

    Fill in your [emergency card](../00-start/emergency-card.md).

## Go to the ER immediately for…

- **Difficulty breathing** — gasping, blue/grey gums, neck extended
- **Collapse, seizures, or unresponsiveness**
- **Pale, white, or blue gums** (a sign of shock or oxygen problems)
- **A bloated, hard abdomen** with retching or restlessness — see [Bloat](#bloat)
- **Repeated vomiting / bloody or black diarrhea**, especially under-vaccinated — see [Parvo](#parvo)
- **Suspected poisoning** — see [Poisoning](#poison)
- **Trauma** — hit by car, big fall, dog-fight bite (internal injury can hide)
- **Can't urinate / straining with nothing produced**
- **Heatstroke** — heavy panting, drooling, wobbly, collapse after heat/exercise
- **Severe bleeding, eye injuries, or sudden facial swelling/hives**

## Bloat (GDV) { #bloat }

**Gastric dilatation-volvulus** is a fast, life-threatening emergency where the stomach fills with gas and can twist. It's **most common in large, deep-chested breeds** (Great Danes, Standard Poodles, German Shepherds, Weimaraners, Setters, Boxers).

**Signs:** a swollen/hard belly, **unproductive retching** (trying to vomit with little coming up), drooling, pacing/restlessness, and signs of pain or distress. **This is minutes-matter urgent — go now.**

**Lower the risk:** feed measured meals (not one huge daily meal), avoid vigorous exercise right around eating, and discuss preventive options with your vet for at-risk breeds.

## Parvovirus { #parvo }

**Parvo** is a highly contagious, potentially fatal virus that hits **unvaccinated and incompletely vaccinated puppies** hardest, and survives in the environment for a long time.

**Signs:** severe **lethargy**, **vomiting**, and **profuse, often bloody, foul-smelling diarrhea**, loss of appetite, and fever — usually in a puppy not yet through the [vaccine series](../02-first-weeks/vaccines.md). It causes rapid, dangerous dehydration.

**What to do:** treat it as an emergency and **call ahead** — clinics isolate suspected parvo to protect other patients. With prompt, aggressive treatment, many puppies recover. This is *why* we complete the vaccine series and limit exposure to risky places until then.

## Poisoning { #poison }

If your puppy ate something toxic (chocolate, **xylitol/birch sugar**, grapes/raisins, onions/garlic, human meds, rat bait, antifreeze, a toxic plant):

1. **Stay calm and act fast.** Remove any remaining product so they can't eat more.
2. **Call** your vet or **poison control** (numbers above). Have the **item, amount, time eaten, and your puppy's weight** ready; keep the packaging.
3. **Do not** induce vomiting unless a professional tells you to — some substances cause more harm coming back up.
4. **Follow their instructions** and go in if directed.

See the full list in [Good vs. bad](../02-first-weeks/good-and-bad.md).

## Choking & swallowed objects

If your puppy is **pawing at the mouth, gagging, or struggling to breathe**, look in the mouth and remove a visible object only if you can do so safely (don't push it deeper). For a collapsing puppy, head to the ER immediately. Many swallowed items (socks, toys, corn cobs, bones) don't choke but cause **intestinal blockage** — repeated vomiting, no stool, a painful belly, and lethargy mean a same-day vet trip.

## A simple first-aid kit

Keep on hand: gauze and self-adhesive bandage wrap, **styptic powder** (for nail bleeds), saline eye wash, a digital thermometer, tweezers/tick remover, a muzzle or strip of fabric (hurting dogs may bite), an e-collar, your vet/ER/poison numbers, and a copy of medical records. Ask your vet before keeping any medications or **3% hydrogen peroxide** — only use the latter to induce vomiting **if specifically instructed.**

## The mindset

You don't need to memorize all of this. You need to **recognize "something is seriously wrong"** and **know who to call.** That, plus a saved set of phone numbers, is what saves puppies.
