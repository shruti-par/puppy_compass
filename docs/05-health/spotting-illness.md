# Is this normal? Spotting illness

Most of catching illness early is just **knowing your puppy's normal** and noticing when it shifts. Spend a week quietly observing the baseline below, and your instincts will do the rest.

## Know the baseline

| Sign | Typical normal (puppy) |
| --- | --- |
| **Gums** | Bubble-gum pink, moist; press and color returns in ~2 seconds |
| **Temperature** | ~**101–102.5 °F** (higher than humans) — taken rectally |
| **Resting heart rate** | ~**120–160+ bpm** (faster in small/young pups) |
| **Resting breathing** | ~**15–40 breaths/min** at rest |
| **Appetite & energy** | Eager to eat; bursts of play then hard naps |
| **Stool** | Formed, brown, consistent |
| **Gait** | Even, no limping or stiffness |

Learn what *your* puppy's gums, energy, and stool look like when healthy — that's your reference point.

## Often-normal puppy things

- **Hiccups, sneezing fits, and the evening "zoomies."**
- A **day of softer stool** after a diet change or stress (and feeding new treats).
- **Mild lethargy for ~24 hours** after vaccines.
- **Sore gums, drooling, and chewing** during teething (~3–6 months).
- A single **vomit** of grass or food in an otherwise bright, playful puppy.

## Call your vet (same day or soon)

- **Diarrhea or vomiting lasting more than ~24 hours**, or any in a young/under-vaccinated puppy
- **Not eating or drinking** for ~12+ hours (sooner for toy breeds — see hypoglycemia below)
- **Persistent coughing, sneezing, or nasal/eye discharge**
- **Limping** that doesn't resolve with rest, or reluctance to move
- **Excessive scratching, hair loss, hot spots, or ear odor/discharge**
- **Changes in urination** — straining, frequency, blood, or housetraining regression
- **Worms in stool**, scooting, or a pot-bellied look (see [Parasites](parasites.md))
- **Lumps, swelling, or anything that just seems "off"** — you know your puppy

!!! warning "Toy-breed hypoglycemia"
    **Very small/toy puppies** can develop dangerously **low blood sugar**,
    especially if they miss meals, get chilled, or are stressed. Signs:
    wobbliness, weakness, glazed eyes, trembling, or collapse. Rubbing a little
    honey or corn syrup on the gums can buy time — then **call your vet
    immediately.** Prevent it with frequent small meals.

## Dehydration check

Gently lift the skin at the scruff — it should **spring right back.** If it stays "tented," or gums are dry/tacky, your puppy may be dehydrated; combined with vomiting or diarrhea, that's a reason to be seen promptly (puppies dehydrate fast).

## Trust the pattern, not a single moment

One odd sneeze isn't a diagnosis. A **cluster** of changes — off food *and* low energy *and* a soft stool — or **any single severe sign** is your cue to act. When you're unsure, a phone call to your vet costs nothing and settles your nerves.
