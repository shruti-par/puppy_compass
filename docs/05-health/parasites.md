# Parasites & prevention

Parasites are nearly universal in puppies and almost entirely **preventable** with a simple year-round routine. Some also infect people, so this is about your family's health too. The good news: a monthly preventive and a couple of vet checks handle most of it.

## Intestinal worms

Puppies are very commonly born with or quickly pick up worms, which is why [deworming](../02-first-weeks/vaccines.md#deworming) starts at ~2 weeks and repeats.

- **Roundworms & hookworms:** the most common; can cause a pot-bellied look, poor growth, diarrhea, and vomiting. Both can infect humans (a reason for hygiene and prompt poop pickup).
- **Whipworms** and **tapeworms** (the latter usually from swallowing a flea or hunting) also occur.

**Signs:** visible worms or rice-like segments in stool or around the rear, scooting, a dull coat, a swollen belly, diarrhea, or weight loss despite eating. Bring **fecal samples** to vet visits — some worms aren't visible.

## Heartworm

Spread by mosquitoes, heartworm is **serious, expensive and difficult to treat, but very easy to prevent.**

- Start **monthly prevention early** — many products are labeled from ~**8 weeks** — and give it **year-round** (mosquitoes appear in most regions for much of the year).
- Many heartworm preventives **also cover intestinal worms**, doing two jobs at once.
- Dogs are tested for heartworm before starting/continuing prevention as they get older.

## Fleas

- **Signs:** scratching, "flea dirt" (black specks that turn red on a wet paper towel), red bumps, hair loss. Some dogs are allergic to flea bites and react intensely.
- **Prevention:** a vet-recommended monthly (or longer) preventive. Treat the **environment** too if you find an infestation — wash bedding, vacuum thoroughly — since most of a flea population lives off the dog.

!!! warning "Use dog-appropriate, age-appropriate products"
    Always check that a flea/tick product is **labeled for your puppy's age and
    weight**, and **never use a dog product on a cat** (some are toxic to
    cats). When in doubt, ask your vet rather than grabbing the strongest
    drugstore option.

## Ticks

- Risk is highest in **wooded, grassy, and brushy areas**, and rising in many regions.
- Ticks transmit diseases like **Lyme, ehrlichiosis, and anaplasmosis.**
- **Prevention:** a vet-recommended tick preventive, plus a **tick check** after walks in tick country (feel around ears, neck, armpits, groin, between toes).
- **Removal:** grasp close to the skin with fine tweezers or a tick tool and pull straight out steadily; clean the area. Don't burn or twist. If a tick was attached a while, mention it to your vet.

## Mange & other skin parasites

**Mites** cause mange (hair loss, intense itching, crusty skin) and ear mites cause dark, crumbly ear discharge and head-shaking. These need veterinary diagnosis and treatment — don't guess with over-the-counter products.

## Your simple prevention routine

- [ ] **Year-round monthly** broad-spectrum preventive (heartworm + intestinal worms; many combine flea/tick too) — chosen with your vet.
- [ ] A separate **flea & tick** preventive if not included.
- [ ] **Fecal checks** at vet visits and deworming on schedule.
- [ ] **Prompt poop pickup** and good hand-washing (protects people too).
- [ ] **Tick checks** after outings in risky areas.

That routine, plus the [vaccine series](../02-first-weeks/vaccines.md), covers the large majority of preventable puppy illness.
