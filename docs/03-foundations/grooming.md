# Daily grooming & hygiene

Grooming does two jobs at once: it keeps your puppy healthy, and — if you start now, gently, with treats — it teaches them to **accept handling** so that nail trims, baths, and vet exams are calm for life. The habit matters as much as the task. Start young, go slow, reward generously.

!!! tip "Build the handling habit first"
    Every day, casually touch your puppy's **paws, ears, mouth, and tail** and
    give a treat. This "consent and reward" handling makes everything below
    easier and turns the groomer and vet into non-events.

## Coat & brushing (breed-specific)

How often and how you brush depends entirely on coat type:

- **Short, smooth coats** (Labrador, Beagle, Boxer): a quick brush **weekly** with a rubber curry or bristle brush; they shed but mat rarely.
- **Double coats** (Husky, German Shepherd, Golden Retriever): brush **2–3× weekly**, daily during seasonal "blowing coat." Use an undercoat rake/slicker. **Never shave a double coat** — it can damage regrowth and the coat's insulation.
- **Long/silky coats** (Yorkie, Maltese, Shih Tzu): brush **daily** to prevent mats, plus regular professional trims.
- **Curly/wool coats** (Poodle, Doodles, Bichon): brush/comb **daily to the skin** — these mat fast and need professional grooming every **4–8 weeks.**
- **Wire coats** (many terriers, Schnauzers): regular brushing plus periodic hand-stripping or clipping.

Get puppies used to the **brush, clippers, and dryer sounds** early with treats, even before they truly need grooming.

### Bathing

Most puppies need a bath only **every 4–6 weeks or when dirty** — over-bathing strips natural oils and dries the skin. Use a **dog-specific shampoo** (human and even baby shampoo is the wrong pH), lukewarm water, avoid the eyes and ears, and dry thoroughly (especially double and curly coats, and skin folds).

## Teeth (yes, daily-ish)

Dental disease is one of the most common health problems in dogs, and it starts young.

- **Brush with a dog toothbrush (or finger brush) and dog toothpaste.** **Never use human toothpaste** — fluoride and especially xylitol are toxic to dogs.
- Aim for **daily**, but even **2–3× a week** helps enormously. Build up slowly: let them taste the (poultry-flavored) paste, then touch the brush to teeth, then brush a little more each day.
- **Dental chews and toys** help but don't replace brushing.

!!! note "Teething timeline"
    Puppies lose their baby teeth and get 42 adult teeth between about **3 and
    6–7 months.** Expect chewing, a little bleeding from the gums, and the odd
    found tooth. Tell your vet if a **baby tooth doesn't fall out** as the adult
    comes in (retained deciduous teeth sometimes need removal, often done at
    spay/neuter).

## Nails

Long nails aren't just cosmetic — they push the toes out of alignment and can make walking painful. If you **hear clicking on hard floors**, they're too long.

- Trim **every 2–4 weeks**, taking off just the tips.
- **Avoid the quick** — the pink (in clear nails) or live core of the nail. Cut tiny slivers; with dark nails, stop when you see a grey/pink oval on the cut surface. Keep **styptic powder** on hand in case you nick it.
- A **nail grinder** is easier for many people and many dogs than clippers — and gentler to introduce.
- **Don't forget dewclaws** (the "thumb" higher on the leg) if your dog has them.

Make nails a relaxed, treat-filled ritual; do **one or two nails** at a time if that's all your puppy tolerates at first.

## Ears & eyes

- **Ears:** check weekly. A little wax is fine; **redness, a yeasty/foul smell, dark discharge, or head-shaking/scratching** means a vet visit (likely an infection). Floppy-eared and hairy-eared breeds (Cockers, Poodles, Basset Hounds) are more prone and may need routine cleaning with a vet-recommended solution. Keep water out of ears during baths.
- **Eyes:** wipe away normal discharge with a damp cloth. **Squinting, redness, excessive tearing, or thick/colored discharge** warrants a check.

## A simple routine

- **Daily-ish:** quick brush (per coat type), tooth brushing, a glance at ears/eyes, and casual paw handling.
- **Weekly:** thorough brush, ear check.
- **Every 2–4 weeks:** nails.
- **Every 4–8 weeks:** bath and/or professional groom (coat-dependent).
