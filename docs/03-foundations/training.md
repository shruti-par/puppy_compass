# Training the basics

Training a puppy is less about obedience and more about **communication.** You're teaching a different species what works to get the good stuff in life. The modern, evidence-backed approach is **positive reinforcement** — reward what you want, prevent and redirect what you don't — and it builds a confident dog who *wants* to work with you.

## The four principles that make it click

1. **Reward what you like, the instant it happens.** Dogs repeat what pays. A treat or praise within a second of the behavior teaches fastest.
2. **Keep sessions tiny.** **2–5 minutes, several times a day** beats one long drill. Puppy attention spans are short; end while they're still winning.
3. **Be consistent.** Same words, same rules, every family member. "Off" can't mean "get down" for you and "it's fine" for someone else.
4. **Set them up to succeed.** Manage the environment so the wrong choice is hard and the right one is easy. Prevention is training.

!!! warning "Skip the aversive stuff"
    Avoid yelling, "alpha rolls," scruff-shakes, and shock/prong/choke
    corrections. Research and the major veterinary behavior organizations link
    punishment-based methods to **more** fear and aggression, not less. Reward-
    based training is both kinder and more effective.

## First skills, in a sensible order

- **Name recognition** — say the name, reward the look. The foundation of everything.
- **Marker word or clicker** — a "yes!" or click that means "that's it, treat coming."
- **Sit** — lure with a treat over the nose; reward the moment the bottom hits the floor.
- **Recall ("come")** — the most important safety skill. Make coming to you *always* rewarding; never call them to something unpleasant.
- **Down, and a "settle"/place** — teaches calm.
- **Leash manners** — reward walking near you; stop when they pull so pulling never "works."
- **"Leave it" / "drop it"** — protects them from swallowing dangerous things.
- **Crate cue** — covered in [Crate training](../02-first-weeks/crate-training.md).

## Three behaviors every puppy does (and how to handle them)

### Nipping and "land-shark" mouthing

Totally normal — puppies explore and play with their mouths, especially while teething. To curb it: **redirect** the mouth to a chew toy every time, **yelp or freeze and end play** for a few seconds when teeth touch skin (no fun happens when biting happens), and make sure they're **not overtired** (overtired puppies bite more — enforce naps). Never use your hands as toys.

### Chewing

Also normal, and intense during **teething (~3–6 months)**. Provide a rotation of appropriate chews, keep valuables out of reach, freeze a wet washcloth or chew toy for sore gums, and praise chewing the *right* things. You're managing it, not eliminating it.

### Jumping up

Puppies jump to greet faces. Reward **four-on-the-floor**: ignore/turn away from jumping, give attention the moment paws are down. Teach an alternative like "sit to say hi." Ask visitors to do the same — inconsistency is what keeps jumping alive.

## When and how to get help

- **Puppy classes** are gold — they combine training with the [safe socialization](socialization.md) you need anyway. Look for classes using positive methods that require proof of vaccination.
- For anything that worries you — real aggression, severe fear, intense separation distress — talk to your vet and consider a **certified positive-reinforcement trainer** or a **veterinary behaviorist** early. Early help is easier help.

!!! tip "Size note"
    With **large breeds**, train manners like "no jumping" and loose-leash
    walking *now*, while they're small — habits set young scale up with the
    dog. With **toy breeds**, take training just as seriously; "small dog
    syndrome" is usually just an under-trained dog, not a personality.
