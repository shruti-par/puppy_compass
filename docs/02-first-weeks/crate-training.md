# Crate training

A crate isn't a cage — done right, it's a **bedroom**: a safe, cozy den your puppy chooses to relax in. It makes potty training faster, keeps your puppy safe when you can't supervise, gives them a calm place to decompress, and makes vet visits, travel, and recovery from surgery far less stressful later. The key is that the crate must always feel *good*.

## Size it correctly

The crate should be **just big enough to stand up, turn around comfortably, and lie down** — and no bigger. Too much space lets a puppy potty in one end and sleep in the other, defeating the housetraining benefit.

!!! tip "Size note"
    For a **large-breed puppy**, buy the **adult-size crate** and use the
    **divider panel** to keep the usable space puppy-sized now, expanding it as
    they grow. For a **toy breed**, a small crate is right for life. The crate
    should never be so cramped they can't turn around.

## Make it wonderful (the first week)

Go slow. You're building a positive association, not forcing confinement.

1. **Set it up** in a calm corner of a family room — near people, not isolated. Add comfy washable bedding.
2. **Door open, treats inside.** Toss treats and toys in and let them wander in and out freely. No closing yet.
3. **Feed meals in the crate** (door open at first). Eating in a place makes it safe.
4. **Add a cue** like "crate" or "bed" as they go in, then reward.
5. **Close the door for seconds, then minutes,** while they're chewing a stuffed toy. Open before they panic. Slowly extend.
6. **Pair it with a chew** — a stuffed, frozen food toy turns crate time into the best part of the day.

!!! warning "Never use the crate as punishment"
    If the crate becomes "the place I get sent when I'm bad," it stops being a
    safe den and the whole system breaks. Keep every association positive.

## How long is too long?

The crate is for sleep and short stretches, not all-day storage. A loose guide for **daytime** crating:

| Age | Reasonable daytime stretch |
| --- | --- |
| 8–10 weeks | Up to ~30–60 min |
| 11–14 weeks | ~1–3 hours |
| 15–16 weeks | ~3–4 hours |
| 4–6 months | ~4–5 hours |
| Adult | No more than ~6–8 hours, with breaks |

Overnight is different — puppies sleep longer at night than they can "hold it" awake, and they grow into sleeping through. Always provide a potty break when they wake and cry.

## Night-time setup

Put the crate **beside your bed** for the first weeks. Hearing and smelling you reassures the puppy and lets you hear genuine potty needs. You can gradually move it to its permanent spot once nights settle. A worn t-shirt, white noise, and a not-too-late dinner all help.

## Common bumps

- **Crying when the door closes:** you moved too fast. Back up a step, build duration more slowly, always reward calm.
- **Won't settle:** they may be under-exercised *or* overtired. A short sniffy walk or a "settle" routine often does more than more play.
- **Soiling the crate:** the crate may be too big (use a divider), they were left too long, or there's a medical issue — check with your vet.
