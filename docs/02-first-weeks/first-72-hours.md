# The first 72 hours

Your puppy has just left everything it has ever known — its mother, littermates, and the only smells it recognizes — and landed in a strange new world with strange new giants. The goal of the first few days isn't to teach anything. It's to help a small animal feel **safe.**

## The ride home

- Bring a **towel or blanket** (ideally one rubbed on the litter so it smells like home) and a helper to hold the puppy, or use a secured crate/carrier.
- Expect possible **car-sickness or whimpering** — it's normal. Stay calm; your energy is contagious.
- Take them to **potty** as soon as you arrive, before going inside.

## Keep day one small

Resist the urge to invite everyone over. A flood of new people and rooms is too much at once.

- Confine them to **one or two puppy-proofed rooms** plus their crate.
- Let them **explore at their own pace.** Sit on the floor and let them come to you.
- Introduce the **crate as a good place** from minute one — toss treats in, feed meals near or in it (see [Crate training](crate-training.md)).
- Start the **potty routine immediately** — out after every nap, meal, and play session (see [Potty training](potty-training.md)).

!!! tip "Size note"
    **Toy-breed puppies** are at real risk of **low blood sugar
    (hypoglycemia)** in the stress of a new home — shakiness, wobbliness, or
    lethargy. Feed small meals frequently and ask your vet about keeping a
    high-calorie gel on hand. **Large-breed puppies** tire fast despite their
    size; don't over-exercise growing joints.

## The first night (it's the hard one)

Almost every puppy cries the first few nights. They're alone for the first time in their life. You have two humane options, and both work:

- **Crate beside your bed.** They can hear and smell you, which is reassuring. A hand dangled near the crate often settles them.
- **A confined safe space** with the crate door open inside an ex-pen.

Things that help: a **warm (safe) snuggle item**, a **worn t-shirt** that smells like you, a late-evening potty trip, and **not** feeding a big meal right before bed. Quiet white noise can mask startling household sounds.

!!! warning "The night-crying trap"
    A puppy crying at 2 a.m. usually needs **either comfort or a potty trip** —
    young puppies physically can't hold it all night. Take them out calmly and
    quietly (no playtime), then back to bed. The mistake is making a party of
    it; the other mistake is ignoring a genuine potty need. With consistency,
    most puppies sleep through within a couple of weeks.

## Set the rhythm

By day three, aim for a loose, repeating cycle: **potty → eat → play/explore → potty → nap (in the crate)**, all day. Puppies this age sleep **18–20 hours a day** — overtiredness looks exactly like "hyper and bitey," so protect nap time fiercely.

## What's normal vs. not in week one

- **Normal:** some hiding, a smaller appetite for a day, soft stool from stress/diet change, lots of sleep, hiccups, the "witching hour" zoomies in the evening.
- **Call the vet:** repeated vomiting, watery or bloody diarrhea, refusing all food/water for more than ~12 hours (sooner for toy breeds), extreme lethargy, or any [emergency sign](../05-health/emergencies.md).
