# The first vet visit

You lined up a vet back in [Choosing a vet](../01-before-home/choosing-a-vet.md). Now the visit itself — what happens, what to bring, and how to make it a positive experience your puppy won't dread for the rest of their life.

## Make it a happy place

The hidden goal of the first visit is **emotional**: you want your puppy to learn that the vet is a treat dispenser, not a scary place. Bring high-value treats, ask the staff to feed them, keep your own energy upbeat, and let the puppy explore the exam room. A "fear-free" clinic will do most of this for you.

## What to bring

- **Medical records** from the breeder/shelter (vaccines and deworming already given).
- A **fresh stool sample** (collected within ~12 hours).
- Your **written question list** (see below).
- A **hungry puppy** + treats, so food rewards land.

## What the vet will do

1. **Weight + full physical exam** — heart and lungs (listening for a murmur), eyes and ears, mouth and bite, abdomen, joints and gait, skin and coat, and a check for hernias or retained baby teeth.
2. **Review and plan vaccines** — what's been done and what's next; build the schedule (see [Vaccines](vaccines.md)).
3. **Fecal exam + deworming** — puppies are very commonly born with intestinal worms.
4. **Parasite prevention plan** — heartworm, flea, and tick prevention appropriate to your region.
5. **Microchip** (if needed) and registration.
6. **Nutrition check** — confirming the right food and amount for projected adult size.
7. **Spay/neuter timing discussion** — start it early; the right age is now size- and breed-specific.

## Your question list

Copy this (a [printable version](../06-tools/checklists.md) is in Tools):

- [ ] Is my puppy a **healthy weight and body condition**?
- [ ] Is the **food and amount** right for their projected adult size?
- [ ] What's our **vaccine and deworming schedule** from here?
- [ ] Which **parasite preventives** do you recommend, and when do we start?
- [ ] What's the right **spay/neuter age** for this breed and size?
- [ ] How should I handle **teething and chewing**?
- [ ] Is there anything I should know about **this breed's** common issues?
- [ ] What are **early warning signs** I should watch for, and when do I call you vs. go to the ER?
- [ ] Do you recommend **pet insurance**, and any tips on choosing it?

## After the visit

- Some puppies are a little **tired or sore** after vaccines — that's normal for a day. **Call** if you see facial swelling, hives, repeated vomiting, or collapse (a rare allergic reaction).
- Put the **next appointment** on your calendar before you leave; the puppy series runs every 2–4 weeks for a while.
