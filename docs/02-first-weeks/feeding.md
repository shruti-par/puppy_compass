# Feeding: how much & what

Feeding a puppy well is mostly about three questions: **what** to feed, **how much**, and **how often** — and all three change with age and adult size. Let's make it concrete.

## What to feed

- Choose a **complete and balanced puppy food** (or "all life stages") that meets **AAFCO** nutritional standards — look for the AAFCO statement on the bag.
- **Match the food to adult size.** Large- and giant-breed puppies should eat a food formulated **"for large-breed puppies."** This isn't marketing: these formulas control calcium and calories to slow bone growth to a healthy rate, which **reduces the risk of orthopedic disease.** Overfeeding a big puppy a rich food is one of the most common, most preventable mistakes.
- **Wet, dry, or both** is mostly preference; dry kibble is convenient and good for teeth, wet is palatable and hydrating. Both are fine if complete and balanced.

!!! warning "About raw, grain-free, and boutique diets"
    Talk to your vet before feeding raw (pathogen and balance risks for a
    growing puppy) or boutique/grain-free diets, some of which the FDA has
    investigated in connection with a heart condition (DCM). For most owners,
    a mainstream complete-and-balanced puppy food from an established maker is
    the safe default.

!!! note "How the estimate works"
    It uses the standard veterinary formula: resting energy
    (RER = 70 × kg<sup>0.75</sup>) multiplied by a growth factor — about **3×**
    for puppies under 4 months and **2×** for older puppies. It's a *starting
    point*, not a prescription. Lean body condition is the real target: you
    should feel the ribs easily and see a waist from above.

## How often

| Age | Meals per day |
| --- | --- |
| 8–12 weeks | **4** small meals |
| 3–6 months | **3** meals |
| 6–12 months | **2** meals |
| Adult | 2 meals (most dogs) |

!!! tip "Size note: when to switch to adult food"
    Switch from puppy to adult food **when growth slows**, which depends on
    size:

    - **Toy/small** (under ~25 lb): around **9–12 months**
    - **Medium** (25–50 lb): around **12 months**
    - **Large/giant** (over ~50 lb): **18–24 months** — they grow for much
      longer, so they need puppy nutrition longer.

## Transitioning foods (do it slowly)

Any food change — including switching to adult food — should happen over **7–10 days** to avoid an upset stomach: mostly old food at first, gradually shifting to mostly new. A common ratio progression is 75/25 → 50/50 → 25/75 → 100% new.

## Free-feeding? No.

Leave food down for **15–20 minutes**, then pick it up. Scheduled meals make house­training predictable (what goes in on a schedule comes out on a schedule), prevent picky habits, and let you spot a lost appetite early — often the first sign of illness.
