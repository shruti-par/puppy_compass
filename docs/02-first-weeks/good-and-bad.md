# Good vs. bad: foods & hazards

Puppies will try to eat almost anything. Knowing the difference between "fine," "fine in moderation," and "call poison control" is one of the highest-value things you can memorize as a new owner.

!!! danger "If your puppy ate something toxic"
    Call your vet, **ASPCA Animal Poison Control (888) 426-4435**, or the
    **Pet Poison Helpline (855) 764-7661** right away. **Don't** induce
    vomiting unless told to. Have the item, the amount, and your puppy's weight
    ready.

## Never — genuinely toxic foods

There is no reliably safe amount of these for any size dog. With truly toxic foods like chocolate, xylitol, grapes, and allium-family vegetables, there is no reliably safe amount for any size dog.

| Food | Why it's dangerous |
| --- | --- |
| **Chocolate, coffee, caffeine** | Methylxanthines (theobromine) — vomiting, racing heart, tremors, seizures. Darker = worse. |
| **Xylitol / "birch sugar"** | Xylitol can cause a rapid drop in blood sugar (hypoglycemia) and potentially liver damage. Hidden in sugar-free gum, mints, some peanut butters, baked goods, and toothpaste. **Always check peanut butter labels.** |
| **Grapes & raisins** | Exposure to grapes/raisins can lead to kidney damage. Treat any ingestion as urgent. |
| **Onions, garlic, leeks, chives** | Damage red blood cells (anemia). Garlic is the most potent. Hidden in broths, baby food, seasoning. |
| **Macadamia nuts** | Have caused weakness, depression, vomiting, tremors and hyperthermia in dogs. |
| **Alcohol & raw yeast dough** | Yeast dough can rise and cause gas in the digestive system, which can be painful and can even cause the stomach or intestines to rupture. Alcohol is toxic. |
| **Cooked bones** | Splinter and cause choking or gut perforation. |

Also keep away: avocado (the pit is a blockage risk), large amounts of salt, and anything mouldy.

!!! warning "Beyond the kitchen"
    The most common pet poisons aren't foods at all — **human medications**
    (pain relievers, antidepressants, ADHD meds), **rodenticides**,
    **antifreeze**, and **xylitol products**. Lock them up. See
    [Puppy-proofing](../01-before-home/puppy-proofing.md).

## Toxic plants (a starter list)

Lilies, sago palm, azalea/rhododendron, oleander, foxglove, yew, autumn crocus, dieffenbachia, philodendron, pothos, and aloe. When buying a houseplant, check it against the [ASPCA toxic-plant database](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants) first.

## Yes — generally safe treats (in moderation)

Healthy "people food" treats are fine as long as they stay under **~10% of daily calories** and you skip the seasoning:

- **Fruit:** apple (no seeds), banana, blueberries, watermelon (no rind/seeds), strawberries.
- **Veg:** carrots, green beans, plain cooked pumpkin (great for mild tummy upset), cucumber.
- **Protein:** plain cooked chicken, turkey, or lean beef; plain cooked egg.
- **Other:** plain peanut butter **(xylitol-free)**, a little plain yogurt if tolerated.

!!! tip "Size note"
    A single grape is dangerous for a 5-lb Chihuahua and a 90-lb Labrador
    alike — toxicity isn't always about size. But **treat calories** very much
    are: 10% of a toy breed's daily intake is just a few tiny pieces, while a
    big puppy can have more. Tiny dogs gain weight on "just a bite" fast.

## Choking & blockage hazards

Beyond food, the things puppies most often swallow and get into trouble with: **socks and underwear, corn cobs, fruit pits, kids' toys, hair ties, string/floss, rocks, and bones.** Many of these need surgery to remove. Supervise, crate when you can't, and pick up the floor.
