# Potty training

House­training is not complicated, but it is relentless in the early weeks. The whole method fits in one sentence: **prevent accidents, reward outdoor success, and be boringly consistent.** Do that and most puppies are reliably trained in a few weeks to a couple of months.

## How puppy bladders work

A rough rule: a puppy can hold it for about **one hour per month of age**, plus one — so a 2-month-old maxes out around **2–3 hours** (less when active), and needs to go right after sleeping, eating, drinking, and playing. Overnight is the exception they grow into.

!!! tip "Size note"
    **Toy and small breeds** have tiny bladders and faster metabolisms — they
    need more frequent trips and often take a bit longer to fully train. Cold
    or rainy weather makes little dogs reluctant to go outside, so consider a
    covered potty spot. **Large breeds** can usually hold it longer sooner,
    but don't rush them.

## The routine

Take your puppy out **on a leash, to the same spot,** at all of these moments:

- First thing in the morning and last thing at night
- After **every** nap, meal, drink, and play session
- Roughly **every 1–2 hours** in between when awake

Then:

1. **Wait** quietly — don't play, just let them sniff and circle.
2. The instant they finish, **reward immediately** — praise + a small treat *right there*, not back inside. Timing is everything.
3. **Then** have your fun walk or playtime as a bonus.

## Supervise or contain — always

When you can't watch every second, your puppy should be **in the crate or a small contained area.** A puppy roaming free is a puppy practicing accidents. Use the crate's natural "don't soil the den" instinct (see [Crate training](crate-training.md)) and tether or gate when you're busy.

## Accidents (they will happen)

- **Interrupt, don't punish.** If you catch them going, a calm "oops!" and a quick trip outside. **Never** scold after the fact or rub their nose in it — they can't connect it, and it only teaches them to hide and fear you.
- **Clean with an enzymatic cleaner**, not regular soap. Dogs return to spots that still smell faintly like a toilet; enzymes remove the scent entirely.

## Pads, bells, and signals

- **Pee pads / indoor grass** can help apartment dwellers, toy breeds, and the pre-vaccination weeks — but they teach "indoors is okay," which can slow full outdoor training. Use deliberately.
- **Bell-training** (hang bells on the door, reward nose/paw touches before going out) gives your puppy a way to *tell* you. Optional but handy.

## Night-time

Young puppies usually **can't** last all night at first. Limit water an hour or two before bed, do a last potty trip right before lights-out, and take them out (calmly, no play) when they wake and cry. They'll stretch through the night within a few weeks.

!!! warning "When 'accidents' might be medical"
    Sudden frequent urination, straining, blood in the urine, or a previously
    trained puppy regressing can signal a **urinary tract infection** or other
    issue. Mention it to your vet rather than assuming it's a training problem.

## Roughly how long?

Expect **basic reliability in 2–8 weeks** of consistent effort, with the occasional accident up to ~6 months as bladder control matures. Consistency from every family member is what makes or breaks the timeline.
