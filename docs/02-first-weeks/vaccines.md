# Vaccines & deworming

Vaccines are the reason your puppy will (almost certainly) sail through the dangerous diseases that once killed huge numbers of dogs. The schedule looks complicated, but the logic is simple: a puppy's borrowed immunity from mom fades unpredictably, so we give a **series** of shots to cover the gap until their own immune system takes over.

!!! tip "Build your personalized schedule"
    The **[Vaccine & deworming planner](../06-tools/vaccine-planner.md)** turns

## Core vs. non-core

**Core** vaccines are recommended for every dog. **Non-core** vaccines are chosen with your vet based on lifestyle and where you live.

### Core vaccines

| Vaccine | What it covers | Puppy timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DAPP / DHPP** | **D**istemper, **A**denovirus (hepatitis), **P**arainfluenza, **P**arvovirus | A series starting at **6–8 weeks**, repeated every **2–4 weeks**, with the **final dose at 16 weeks or older.** |
| **Leptospirosis** | A bacterial, zoonotic disease | Now considered **core for all dogs** as of the 2024 AAHA update; given as **two doses starting around 12 weeks**, 2–4 weeks apart. |
| **Rabies** | Rabies virus (fatal, zoonotic) | A single dose between **12 and 16 weeks**, then per your **state's law**. |

The distemper-combination series matters most: puppies generate long-lasting immunity to DAPP/DHPP only after completing the **full series**, with that final dose at 16+ weeks. That's why you can't cut it short.

### Common non-core (by lifestyle/region)

- **Bordetella + canine parainfluenza ("kennel cough")** — if you'll use daycare, boarding, grooming, or group classes (often required).
- **Canine influenza** — for social/boarding dogs; two doses 2–4 weeks apart.
- **Lyme disease** — in tick-heavy regions; two doses 2–4 weeks apart.
- **Rattlesnake toxoid** — in parts of the country with rattlesnakes.

!!! warning "The protection gap (this affects socialization)"
    Your puppy is **not fully protected until about 1–2 weeks after the final
    DAPP dose at 16+ weeks.** Until then, avoid dog parks, pet-store floors,
    and unknown dogs' potty areas — parvo lives in the environment. But you
    **shouldn't** lock your puppy away, because the [socialization
    window](../03-foundations/socialization.md) is closing at the same time.
    The answer is *safe* socialization: known-vaccinated dogs, clean
    environments, carry-and-expose outings, and puppy classes that require
    proof of vaccination. Ask your vet — most now actively recommend this
    balance.

## A typical schedule at a glance

| Age | Usual visit |
| --- | --- |
| **6–8 weeks** | DAPP #1 |
| **10–12 weeks** | DAPP #2, Leptospirosis #1 (± non-core start) |
| **14–16 weeks** | DAPP #3 (final), Leptospirosis #2, Rabies |
| **~1 year** | Boosters for core vaccines |

After the puppy series and 1-year boosters, **core vaccines are typically given every 3 years**, while lepto, Lyme, canine flu, and Bordetella are **annual** if your dog needs them. Exact timing is set by your vet and local law.

## Deworming

Most puppies are born with or quickly pick up intestinal worms (roundworms, hookworms), so deworming starts young and repeats:

- **Every 2 weeks** from about 2 weeks of age until 12 weeks.
- Then **monthly** until about 6 months.
- After that, **year-round broad-spectrum parasite prevention** (many monthly heartworm products also cover intestinal worms).
- Bring a **fecal sample** to vet visits so anything missed gets caught.

Heartworm prevention can usually begin around **8 weeks** (product-dependent) and should be given **year-round** — see [Parasites & prevention](../05-health/parasites.md).

!!! note "Why so many shots?"
    It's not that any one shot is weak — it's that we don't know exactly when
    mom's antibodies fade for *your* puppy. They can block a vaccine from
    "taking." Spacing several doses through ~16 weeks ensures at least one
    lands after maternal antibodies are gone, closing the window of
    vulnerability.
